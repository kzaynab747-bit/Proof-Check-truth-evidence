Why it matters

Suspicious messages are built to rush you.

Most of them follow the same few patterns. Seeing the pattern is often enough to slow down and check.

Job offers

Instant hiring, high pay for simple work, and a fee or document request before any real interview.

Shopping deals

Prices far below normal, countdown timers, and a request to pay in advance to a personal account.

Payment requests

Urgent transfers, requests for codes or card details, and pressure to keep the payment secret.

Privacy

Your message stays on your device.

People are right to be careful about where they paste suspicious messages. ProofCheck does the analysis in your browser, so there is nothing to send anywhere.

Local analysis

Simple text rules run on your device. No account, no sign-in.

Never uploaded

The text you paste is not sent to a server and is not stored.

Still be careful

Do not paste passwords, one-time codes or card numbers into any tool, including this one.

Know the limits

Signals, not verdicts.

ProofCheck is a prototype built on simple text rules. Use it to think, then confirm with the real source.

Warnings are not proof

A warning pattern is a reason to investigate. It does not show that a person or organization is dishonest.

It can miss scams

A carefully worded message may match no rule, and legitimate messages can trigger warnings.

Verify independently

Use contact details from an official website you found yourself, not only those inside the message.

Questions

Frequently asked.

Does ProofCheck say whether a message is a scam?

No. It shows which phrases match known warning patterns and explains them. The decision stays with you, after you verify through an official channel.

Is my message stored or sent anywhere?

No. The analysis runs in your browser and the text is never uploaded. Even so, avoid pasting passwords, one-time codes or card details.

Which languages does it understand?

English and Roman Urdu, which is Urdu written in Latin letters. Urdu script and other languages are not covered yet.

Why did a normal message get flagged?

The checker only reads wording. Real companies also use urgent or promotional phrases, so a flag means "check this", not "this is fake".

What should I do if a message looks risky?

Do not pay or share codes. Find the organization's official website yourself, use the contact details listed there, and ask a trusted person.
